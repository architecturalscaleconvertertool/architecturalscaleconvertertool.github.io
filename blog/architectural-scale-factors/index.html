Architectural Scale Factors: Conversion Chart and Examples

Published October 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Architectural scale factors convert paper measurements to real-world dimensions when both use the same units. Multiply a paper measurement by the factor, or divide a model measurement by it, to move between the sheet and the building. For a scale written as X in = 1 ft-0 in, the factor is 12 divided by X. The charts below cover common architectural scales from 1/32 in to 3 in = 1 ft, followed by engineering scales, with a viewport XP value for each. All XP values assume model space and paper space both use inches.

What is an architectural scale factor?

The scale factor is the ratio of actual size to plotted size, expressed as a single dimensionless number. When a drawing states 1/4 in = 1 ft-0 in, one foot of real building (12 in) is drawn 1/4 in on paper, so the factor is 12 divided by 1/4, which is 48. Architectural scales like these are expressed in feet and inches — ratios such as 1/4 in = 1 ft or 3/8 in = 1 ft — and are the standard notation for building plans, elevations and details.

A factor of 48 means each real-world length is 48 times its plotted length when measured in the same units. Its reciprocal, 1/48, converts model dimensions to paper dimensions. Keeping that direction clear helps prevent scaling errors.

How do you convert a drawing scale to a scale factor?

Take the inches-per-foot value, invert the fraction, and multiply by 12. That single rule produces every architectural scale factor in the chart.

  1. Select the desired scale, for example 1/8 in = 1 ft-0 in.
  2. Invert the fraction: 1/8 becomes 8/1.
  3. Multiply by 12 (the inches in one foot): 8 x 12 = 96.

A less convenient scale works the same way. For 3/16 in = 1 ft-0 in, invert to get 16/3, then multiply by 12: (16/3) x 12 = 64, which is why the 3/16 scale factor is 64. For an engineering scale written as 1 in = 20 ft, there is no fraction to invert — just multiply the feet by 12, so 20 x 12 = 240. In every case, convert both distances to the same units (inches) before dividing; the resulting factor is dimensionless.

Engineering scale factors (1 in = 10 ft to 1 in = 100 ft)

Engineering scales — typical on civil and site plans — flip the notation to 1 in = N ft, so the scale factor is simply N multiplied by 12. Verify one row yourself, such as 1 in = 50 ft giving 50 x 12 = 600.

How do you apply a scale factor in AutoCAD?

To set a viewport scale in AutoCAD, activate the layout viewport, run ZOOM, and enter the reciprocal factor followed by xp. With inch-based model and paper units, a 1/8 in = 1 ft-0 in view requires 1/96xp, the reciprocal of factor 96. The xp suffix makes this zoom value relative to paper space; entering 96xp would enlarge the view instead. For text heights and DIMSCALE settings, see the chart of CAD scale factors.

Beyond viewport zooms, you can scale geometry with the SCALE command by specifying a reference length and the desired size, apply factors globally or to individual elements, and manage the preset scale list with SCALELISTEDIT. Whichever route you take, double-check units before applying a factor, since a multiplier and its reciprocal are not interchangeable.

Common mistakes when working with scale factors

Check for three errors before plotting: using the wrong formula, mixing units, and manually resizing annotation that already scales automatically.

  • Using the wrong formula family: 12 divided by X belongs to architectural scales (X in = 1 ft); engineering scales (1 in = N ft) need N times 12. Using 12/20 instead of 20 x 12 for a 1 in = 20 ft site plan produces a drawing 400 times off.
  • Mixing units: the factor is actual inches divided by paper inches, so always convert feet to inches before dividing.
  • Manually resizing annotative content: annotative dimensions use the current annotation scale to control their displayed size. Do not apply the traditional model-space text-height multiplication to annotative dimensions.

To check architectural scale factors before plotting, multiply the factor by the drawing-scale fraction: the result must equal 12 in per foot. For instance, 192 x 1/16 in = 12 in, so a 1/16 in = 1 ft-0 in drawing correctly carries factor 192.
