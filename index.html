Convert Architectural Scale to Actual Size in Feet and Inches

To convert an architectural scale to actual size, multiply the length measured on the drawing by the scale ratio: 3 1/4″ measured at 1/4″ = 1′-0″ (ratio 48) is 3.25 × 48 = 156″, or 13′-0″. Convert Inches or Millimeters to Architectural Scale

Inches convert to an architectural scale by dividing the real length by the scale ratio: a 15′-0″ wall (180″) at 1/4″ = 1′-0″ is 180 ÷ 48 = 3 3/4″ on paper. Millimeters take one extra step, since 3,810 mm is 150″ (3,810 ÷ 25.4) and 150 ÷ 48 gives 3 1/8″. The converter accepts 3,810 mm as typed.

Convert Drawing Scales from One Scale to Another

To convert drawing scales from one scale to another, divide the second number of the old ratio by the second number of the new one and print at that percentage: going from 1/8″ = 1′-0″ (1:96) to 1/4″ = 1′-0″ (1:48) is 96 ÷ 48 = 2, a 200% print. The percentage applies to length, not area: a 200% print makes every line twice as long and covers four times the area. A full 24 × 36 sheet enlarged to 200% needs 48 × 72 in of paper, so only the part of the sheet in use is worth enlarging.

Most tools take the percentage as typed, with one exception:

  • Print dialog: In Adobe Acrobat's print dialog, choose Custom Scale and type the percentage.

  • Copier: On a copier, set the zoom or reduce/enlarge value to the same percentage.

  • CAD plot: In CAD, skip the percentage and plot at the new scale, such as 1/4″ = 1′-0″.

After any rescale, update the scale written on the sheet, since it still names the old scale.

Drawings set up in feet divide the factor by 12, which is why 1″ = 20′ gives 20 rather than 240. The xp values use the paper-space scale format of the ZOOM command in Autodesk's AutoCAD help.

Three of the four values are set in different parts of AutoCAD:

  • Viewport scale: Select the layout viewport and pick the scale from its scale list, or, with the viewport active, enter the xp value in the ZOOM command.

  • DIMSCALE: Enter it in Use overall scale of, on the Fit tab of the Dimension Style Manager, or type DIMSCALE at the command line.

  • Model-space text height: Enter it as the height of the text style or of the TEXT command.

With annotative text and dimension styles, AutoCAD sizes text and dimensions from each viewport's annotation scale, so DIMSCALE and model-space text height no longer need a separate value for each scale.

Find the Architectural Scale of a Drawing

To find the architectural scale of a drawing, measure one dimension whose real length you know and divide the real length by the measured length: a 20′-0″ wall that measures 5″ on the print gives 240″ ÷ 5″ = 48, a 1:48 ratio, which is 1/4″ = 1′-0″.

The longest written dimension or a grid spacing makes the best reference, since a longer length shrinks the effect of a small measuring error.

A print at true size lands on a standard ratio, while a resized print also needs the reprint percentage the converter shows:

  • True to scale: The ratio lands exactly on 1:48, the print is at 100%, and a 1/4″ scale ruler reads it directly.

  • Resized print: The same wall measured as 4 13/16″ gives a ratio of 1:49.87, so the closest architectural scale is 1/4″ = 1′-0″, the print is at 96.25% of true size, and a 103.9% reprint restores it.

The Compare with menu starts on architectural scales, which suits drawings dimensioned in feet and inches; a drawing dimensioned in millimeters needs the metric or all-scales setting instead.

Frequently Asked Questions About Architectural Scales

What Is an Architectural Scale?

An architectural scale is a drawing ratio written as inches on paper for each foot of building, such as 1/8″ = 1′-0″, which equals 1:96. An architectural scale also names the architect's scale ruler printed with these ratios, usually a three-sided ruler that carries several scales on its faces.

How Do You Calculate Architectural Scale?

You calculate an architectural scale by dividing 12 by the inches on paper that stand for one foot: 3/16″ = 1′-0″ is 12 ÷ 0.1875 = 64, a ratio of 1:64.

Is 1.75 an Actual Architectural Scale?

No, 1.75 is not a standard architectural scale: as 1 3/4″ = 1′-0″ it is about 1:6.86, between the standard 1 1/2″ (1:8) and 3″ (1:4) scales. Typed as 1:75, it works as a custom ratio.

What Is a Scale Factor?

A scale factor is the number you multiply a drawing measurement by to get real size: for 1/2″ = 1′-0″ it is 24. In inch-based CAD drawings the same number sets text and dimension sizes.

What Is the Difference Between Architectural and Engineering Scales?

The difference between architectural and engineering scales is the unit: architectural scales give inches of paper per foot and read in feet and inches, while engineering scales give feet per inch of paper and read in decimal feet.

How Do You Read an Architectural Scale?

To read an architectural scale, use the face marked with the drawing's scale, count whole feet from zero in one direction, and read inches on the divided section past zero. On the 1/4″ face, 3 foot marks plus 6 inch marks read 3′-6″. The U.S. Fire Administration guide Using Engineer and Architect Scales shows the same method.

What Scale Are Architectural Drawings?

Architectural drawings use the scale that fits the sheet and shows the needed detail: floor plans at 1/4″ or 1/8″ = 1′-0″, details at 1 1/2″ or 3″ = 1′-0″, site plans at engineering scales, and physical models at 1:500 to 1:20, depending on what they show. Metric sets use 1:50 or 1:100 for plans and 1:5 to 1:20 for details. The plan and detail ranges match the drawing scale slides of New York City College of Technology (CUNY).

What Is the Most Common Architectural Scale?

The most common architectural scale in US drawing sets is 1/4″ = 1′-0″ (1:48), because a 60′-0″ house is only 15″ long at that scale and still fits a 24 × 36 sheet with room for dimensions and notes.

What Are Common Architectural Scales for Site Plans?

Site plans usually use engineering scales such as 1″ = 20′, 1″ = 30′ or 1″ = 40′, or an architectural 1/32″ or 1/16″ = 1′-0″.

What Scale Are Architectural Models?

Architectural models take their scale from what they must show: site models at 1:500 or 1:1000, building models at 1:200 or 1:100, and interior or detail models at 1:50 or 1:20. Among architectural scales, 1/16″, 1/8″ and 1/4″ = 1′-0″ (1:192, 1:96 and 1:48) sit closest to 1:200, 1:100 and 1:50.

What Is 1/4 Scale in Architecture?

In architecture, 1/4 scale means 1/4″ = 1′-0″, so a quarter inch on the drawing equals one foot of building, a ratio of 1:48, and a 3″ line on the plan is 12′-0″.

Is 1/4 Scale the Same as 1/4 Inch Scale?

No, 1/4 scale and 1/4 inch scale are different: a 1/4 scale model or figure is 1:4, a quarter of real size, while 1/4 inch scale is 1:48, so the model is twelve times larger than a 1/4″ drawing.

What Is 1/8 Scale in Feet?

1/8 scale in architecture means 1/8″ = 1′-0″, a 1:96 ratio, so a 2 1/2″ line on the drawing is 20′-0″ in feet.

What Is 1:100 in Feet and Inches?

At 1:100, one inch on the drawing equals 100 inches, or 8′-4″, so a 3″ line is 25′-0″.

How Do You Convert 1:20 Scale to Full Size?

To convert 1:20 scale to full size, multiply the drawing measurement by 20, so 35 mm on a 1:20 drawing is 700 mm full size.
